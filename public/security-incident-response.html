DecisionPorter — Security Incident Response Policy

Last updated: October 2, 2026 · Reviewed at least once a year

This policy describes how Decision Porter LLC detects, handles and reports security incidents affecting the DecisionPorter app, including any unauthorized access to, or loss of, merchant or customer data.

Roles

  • Incident lead: Edward Rainsford, Founder, Decision Porter LLC. Responsible for every step below and for decisions to notify.
  • Reporting address: support@decisionporter.com. Anyone, including merchants and security researchers, can report a suspected incident here.

1. Detect and record

  • Sources: hosting and error logs, the app's access log, alerts from Render, GitHub, Shopify or connected services, and reports to the address above.
  • Every suspected incident is recorded with the time it was found, what was observed and who reported it.

2. Contain (target: within 24 hours of detection)

  • Stop affected processing: set DISABLE_SCHEDULER=true or suspend the service on Render.
  • Rotate exposed credentials: the Shopify API secret, the database password, and any affected Gorgias, Klaviyo, Inventory Planner or Judge.me keys (merchants are asked to regenerate theirs).
  • If the encryption key may be exposed, generate a new key and re-encrypt stored secrets before restarting.
  • Revoke access for any compromised account and enforce two-factor sign-in on Render, GitHub and Shopify Partners.

3. Assess

  • Determine which stores and which data were affected, using the access log, database records and hosting logs.
  • Decide whether personal data was accessed, changed, lost or disclosed.

4. Notify

  • Affected merchants: without undue delay, and within 72 hours of confirming that their data was affected. The notice states what happened, which data, what we have done and what they should do.
  • Shopify: through Shopify Partner Support, without undue delay, when Shopify merchant or customer data is involved.
  • Authorities or individuals: where the law requires, or to support merchants in meeting their own obligations.

5. Recover

  • Fix the cause, restore from backup if data was lost, verify the fix, and resume processing.

6. Review

  • Within 14 days, write a short post-incident review: timeline, cause, impact, and changes made to prevent a repeat.
  • This policy is reviewed and walked through at least once a year, and after every incident.
